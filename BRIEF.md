# A Baronesa Calçados Brief

## Project

**Name:** A Baronesa Calçados landing page

**Description:** A landing page for a multi-brand shoe store operating out of a single physical location in Lucélia, SP, in continuous operation since 1966. The store carries women's, men's, and children's footwear from several national brands (Vizzano, Beira Rio, Moleca, Modare confirmed; Zaxy unverified). There is no digital ordering or booking platform for this business; every purchase path runs through a human conversation on WhatsApp. The page exists to make the store's real range and character legible online and to route interested visitors into that WhatsApp conversation, not to operate as a priced storefront.

**Target audience:** Local and regional shoppers (women, men, and parents buying children's shoes) in and around Lucélia who already think of footwear shopping as a personal, in-store or WhatsApp interaction, plus the store's existing 4,333-follower Instagram audience being pointed to a more permanent home than a social feed.

**Business goal:** Give a 60-year-old local business a credible, findable web presence that reflects the actual depth of its catalog and converts interest into a WhatsApp conversation. Success is measured in conversations started, not in a self-serve checkout that this business does not have.

---

## The four axes

### Tone Register: Conversational

The store's own voice ("Conforto e tendencia é BARONESA!", "Fale com nossa equipe") is warm, informal, and openly enthusiastic about the merchandise, closer to a shopkeeper who knows your family than an institution; the page should read the same way, on the warmer end of Conversational rather than the flat, restrained end.

### Aesthetic Philosophy: Controlled Maximalist

The store's real photography and Instagram bio both point toward breadth as the actual story (four confirmed brands, three audiences, and categories running from basic sneakers to western boots, combat boots, and rhinestone sandals inside one recent sample), so the page groups and shows that real range with intent rather than compressing it into a single restrained hero image that would understate what the store actually carries.

### Audience Relationship: Companion

A single family-run shop serving women, men, and children across generations, with a stated invitation to "talk to our team," is a relationship built on ongoing personal presence rather than expertise or peer-to-peer exchange, so the page should read as a store that walks alongside a shopper's footwear needs over time, not one instructing or challenging them.

### Sensory Ambition: Considered

The real photography has genuine charm (gold hardware, rhinestone texture, a recurring hand-model detail) but is unpolished catalog photography, not art direction with a built emotional arc, so the page should let visible craft in layout, color, and image selection show without asking the visitor for an emotional payoff the material has not earned.

---

## Weighing the Pé Direito precedent

Loja Pé Direito is a real, comparable precedent (another local multi-brand footwear retailer with no priced digital catalog) and its brief (Conversational, Editorial Restrained, Companion, Considered) is a legitimate starting point, not one to reject on principle. This brief keeps three of those four positions for reasons independent of the precedent: Conversational fits because this store's own bio voice is genuinely warm and informal, Companion fits because this is literally a multi-generation family shop inviting a personal conversation, and Considered fits because the real photography supports visible craft but not a manufactured emotional arc.

The one deliberate departure is Aesthetic Philosophy. Pé Direito's Editorial Restrained register was built on and suits a calm, sparse assortment story told through a few large real photos. A Baronesa's own evidence does not support that same move. The isolated visual read describes the real photography itself as an "unstaged, functional presentation" rather than an editorial one, spanning black leather, white sneakers, beige and cognac tones, and metallic gold and glitter texture across sandals, mules, western boots, and combat boots, a materially louder and more varied set than a calm single-material story can hold. The Instagram bio is also more commercial and exclamatory in register ("Conforto e tendencia é BARONESA!") than a quiet precedent implies, and one sampled real photo already carries a store-made discount graphic, evidence that this specific business already promotes itself in a busier, more catalog-forward way than Pé Direito's calm restraint. Reusing Editorial Restrained here would suppress real, differentiating breadth (four brands, three audiences, a wide category spread) in favor of a register borrowed from a different business's material, so this brief lands on Controlled Maximalist instead: dense with real product photography, but grouped and composed with the same intent Editorial Restrained applies to its single hero image, just applied across more of them.

---

## Synthesis

This brief produces a page that reads like a well-run neighborhood shoe shop, not a generic e-commerce template and not a hushed boutique. Copy speaks in first person plural, warmly and directly, comfortable naming the brands carried and the fact that the store has been open since 1966, without ever naming a price or implying a self-serve checkout exists. The page is organized around real breadth (grouping by audience or category rather than one undifferentiated grid) using the store's own real, unwatermarked photography, so a visitor sees the actual range of what's on the floor: everyday sandals and sneakers alongside western boots, combat boots, and going-out pieces with rhinestone or metallic detail. Every module earns its density; nothing is decorative filler, and nothing pretends to be sparser or calmer than the real catalog is. Every path toward a purchase decision, without exception, ends at a WhatsApp conversation with the actual team, never at a price, a cart, or a checkout flow, because none of those exist for this business.

The page trusts the material it actually has: real photography over stock, an honest 60-year local story over invented brand mythology, and a modest, real 4.7-rating trust signal over any manufactured social proof. It does not try to out-calm a precedent whose calm was earned by a different, sparser catalog.

## Inspiration references

- **Loja Pé Direito (pe-direito-calcados.vercel.app)**: the closest real precedent (same segment, same no-priced-catalog constraint), useful for confirming Conversational, Companion, and Considered, and for showing exactly the density register this brief deliberately does not reuse. See "Weighing the Pé Direito precedent" above.
- **A Baronesa's own Instagram feed and real photography**: the primary reference for this brief, not an outside inspiration. The isolated visual read (grey tile floor, blurred glass storefront, gold hardware, mixed leather and glitter texture, recurring bare-leg model shots) is the actual material the Aesthetic Philosophy axis is built from, and should be treated as the house style rather than styled away from.

## Rejection list

This brief explicitly says no to:

- No price anywhere on the page, at any point, for any item (permanent project rule).
- No navigable priced catalog, cart, or checkout flow; every purchase path ends at a WhatsApp conversation.
- No discount, sale, or price-tag graphics of any kind, including the store's own promotional overlay observed in the raw source photos.
- No watermarks from the raw Instagram source images; use unwatermarked or cropped frames only.
- No invented testimonials, review quotes, or fabricated social proof; the real 4.7-rating, 15-review figure may be used honestly and modestly, never dressed up as more than it is.
- No fake urgency ("only a few left," countdown timers, "selling fast").
- No stock photography of feet, shoes, or models; the store's own real photography is the only imagery source.
- No single-hero-image, mostly-empty layout borrowed wholesale from the Pé Direito precedent; that register belongs to a calmer, sparser catalog than this one.
- No claim that "Lucélia" in the account name refers to a specific person, and no claim about which CNPJ currently operates the store; both are unresolved per the facts document and should stay unnamed or generic in copy.
- No hard commitment to specific weekday closing hours in copy beyond what is confirmed, given the open question about whether the 16:00 weekday close is current.

---

## Open questions

- Whether "Lucélia" in the Instagram display name and hashtag refers only to the city or also to a person tied to the business; unresolved in the facts document, so copy should default to the safer, city-only reading unless the client confirms otherwise.
- Whether the original 1966 CNPJ registration status ("BAIXADA" per third-party aggregators) versus the currently operating business reflects a simple registry restructuring or a stale record; not something this page needs to resolve, but worth keeping out of any copy that makes a specific legal-entity claim.
- Whether "Coleção 2026" is a one-off named line or a recurring annual naming convention; affects whether that phrase can be used as evergreen copy or needs to be treated as dated, time-limited content.
- Whether the store's weekday closing time is really 08:30 to 16:00, given that this is unusually early for retail and unverified beyond the Google Business listing.
- Whether Zaxy is a genuine fifth brand carried by the store; only surfaced in an unverified third-party search snippet, so it should not be listed as a confirmed brand without further verification.
- The choice of underlying page shape (an `ecommerce-standout`-style adaptation is the likely fit, matching the Pé Direito precedent's own reasoning for a local business without a navigable priced catalog) is a separate, downstream structural decision and does not block this brief; palette, tone, and type direction here hold regardless of how that shape question resolves.
